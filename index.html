The AI safety landscape

The active organizations working to make advanced AI safe, arranged by where they apply leverage, from inside the frontier labs outward to funding and public pressure, and colored by where they sit. Each card names the threat model it is built around, its theory of change, whether it does hands-on technical / empirical work, a rough headcount, and links to the org. The subtitle on each card names the main output the world gets if the org succeeds: knowledge, measurements, tools, techniques & standards, or systems on the technical side; policy, talent, funding, or public pressure elsewhere. Where an org's documented record diverges from its self-description, an amber reality check says so. Filters are below.

01

Inside the frontier labs

Safety work embedded in the organizations building the most capable models — the highest-leverage seat, and the one with the most obvious conflict of interest. They all build frontier systems; the split below reflects each lab's stated founding mission and public positioning, which is itself debated.

Founded on a safety mission

Anthropic↗

For-profit · PBC
USSan Francisco · remote · ≈5,000 staff

Systems — frontier models + safety techniques

Technical

Threat model

Models are scaled past our understanding; misalignment and catastrophic misuse emerge at the frontier before mitigations exist.

Theory of change

Stay at the frontier so safety (interpretability, alignment science, evals) is done on real cutting-edge models and ships inside deployed systems; tie scaling to a Responsible Scaling Policy. Signed the voluntary White House Accord on Super Intelligence (29 Sept 2026).

Reality check

Watchdogs document repeated loosening of the RSP: insider-threat safeguards were weakened days before a model launch (Midas Project, 2025), and v3 (effective 24 Feb 2026) dropped the signature pledge to pause scaling if safeguards lagged, recasting some mitigations as industry-wide recommendations. Under v3 — revised repeatedly through 2026 (v3.4) — it publishes Frontier Safety Roadmaps and periodic Risk Reports (second issued Aug 2026). Anthropic argues unilateral binding commitments don't make the world safer absent regulation; critics reply the RSP now reads as aspiration, not constraint.

Safe Superintelligence Inc.↗

For-profit
USPalo Alto / Tel Aviv · remote · ≈50 staff (2025 figure) · $5B from Nvidia

Systems — safe superintelligence, no interim products

Technical

Threat model

Commercial product cycles corrupt safety on the path to superintelligence.

Theory of change

Insulate one team from all market pressure and advance safety and capability together in a single straight shot toward safe superintelligence.

Reality check

Nothing to check yet: the company publishes no research, ships no products, and discloses almost nothing, so its safety approach is currently unverifiable from the outside.

Capability labs with dedicated safety teams

Google DeepMind — AGI Safety↗

For-profit
UKLondon · remote · ≈6,000 staff

Systems — frontier models + safety framework

Technical

Threat model

AGI-level misalignment and dangerous capabilities arising inside a leading lab.

Theory of change

Solve alignment where the most capable models are built, so mitigations ship with them; publish a Frontier Safety Framework (v3.1, 2026) that gates on capability thresholds. Signed the voluntary White House Accord on Super Intelligence (29 Sept 2026).

Reality check

Gemini 2.5 Pro shipped in March 2025 with no model card; a six-page card followed weeks later, and 60 UK lawmakers (in an open letter organized by PauseAI) called the delay a breach of Google's summit transparency pledges; Google also declined to confirm whether external testers like the UK AISI had evaluated the model before release. Google says its testing and reporting fulfilled its commitments; later Gemini releases shipped with fuller safety documentation.

OpenAI — Safety / Preparedness↗

For-profit · PBC
USSan Francisco · remote · ≈4,500 staff (Mar 2026), scaling toward ~8,000

Systems — frontier models, iterative deployment

Technical

Threat model

Loss of control and catastrophic misuse as capability scales rapidly.

Theory of change

Iterative deployment plus a Preparedness Framework: release progressively, learn from contact with reality, and use each model generation to help align the next. Signed the voluntary White House Accord on Super Intelligence (29 Sept 2026).

Reality check

The most documented gap in the field: the Superalignment team dissolved in 2024 amid safety-staff departures — its co-lead said safety culture had "taken a backseat" to products — reporting describes compressed safety-testing timelines, and the company restructured away from nonprofit control while subpoenaing nonprofit critics (incl. The Midas Project). OpenAI says its Preparedness process and external testing continue — and in Aug–Sept 2026 the framework visibly bit: its Astra model was rated “Critical” for cyber capability and OpenAI paused frontier training for about two weeks, the first public case of a lab halting a run on its own risk threshold.

Meta — Superintelligence Labs↗

For-profit
USMenlo Park · remote · MSL safety/risk function

Systems — frontier open-weight models + risk framework

Technical

Threat model

Frontier models — especially released open-weight — could enable catastrophic misuse (CBRN, cyber) or loss of control.

Theory of change

Gate frontier development on catastrophic-risk thresholds via the Meta Superintelligence Scaling Framework (Oct 2026) — chem/bio, cyber and loss of control — with containment applied during training rather than only before release, and oversight at Board level. Ships Llama openly with developer safety tooling.

Reality check

Meta's safety commitment is the most contested of the labs here: it declined to sign the EU General-Purpose AI Code of Practice, its Llama 4 model card was criticized as thin, and its AI org was restructured into Superintelligence Labs in 2025 amid a capabilities push — while its open-weight strategy means safeguards are hard to enforce once weights are public. Meta says its framework and open ecosystem responsibly manage frontier risk.

02

Independent empirical labs

Research and engineering done on real models by groups outside the frontier labs — the core of the technical field, and where hands-on alignment, control, interpretability, and evaluation work concentrates.

Redwood Research↗

Nonprofit
USBerkeley · remote · ≈30 staff · >$70M over 2 yrs

Techniques — control protocols labs can adopt

Technical

Threat model

A model may be misaligned — even scheming — and alignment won't be solved before it is deployed.

Theory of change

Build control measures — monitoring, auditing, containment — that hold even if the model is actively subverting them, and hand labs protocols they can adopt directly. Behind the original AI-control paper, the alignment-faking work with Anthropic, and an AI-control safety case with UK AISI.

METR↗

Nonprofit
USBerkeley · remote · ≈60 staff

Measurements — autonomy & dangerous-capability evals

Technical

Threat model

Autonomy and self-improvement capabilities outrun oversight silently between releases.

Theory of change

Build rigorous third-party evaluations of dangerous capabilities so labs and governments can gate scaling on measured evidence, not vibes.

Apollo Research↗

For-profit · PBC
UKLondon · remote · ≈45 staff

Measurements — scheming & deception evals

Technical

Threat model

Models that strategically deceive and hide their own misalignment from evaluators.

Theory of change

Build scheming and deception evaluations plus the interpretability to support them, then put the evidence in front of labs and regulators before deployment. Its current bet is embedded evaluation — placing evaluators inside the developer's own process, on the argument that external testing cannot be meaningful from outside it.

Parallax↗

Nonprofit
UKCambridge / distributed · ≈8 staff

Knowledge — belief elicitation & white-box agent auditing

Technical

Threat model

Behavioural evals only show what an agent did, not what it believed or was trying to do — so an agent's latent goals and plans stay invisible to the people auditing it.

Theory of change

Build the science of belief elicitation for white-box auditing: how agents encode latent beliefs, goals and plans, and how those shape behaviour — including cognitive-map probes that recover what an agent believes about its environment. A project of Meridian.

Sampura Research↗

Nonprofit
UKLondon

Knowledge — human-AI complementarity for oversight

Technical

Threat model

As models exceed human ability on the tasks we need to supervise, human judgement alone stops being a reliable training and evaluation signal.

Theory of change

Build Human-AI Complementarity for Scalable Oversight — improving the human/AI 'judges' that supervise frontier models. Founded Aug 2026 by Rishub Jain and Joshua Jacob, who left Google DeepMind to pursue the agenda independently.

Palisade Research↗

Nonprofit
USBerkeley · remote · ≈15 staff

Measurements — offensive-capability demonstrations

Technical

Threat model

Decision-makers underestimate concrete offensive AI capabilities (hacking, deception, autonomy).

Theory of change

Build and publicly demonstrate real dangerous capabilities so the risks become legible to policymakers and the public — most recently, language models autonomously hacking and self-replicating.

Robocurve↗

For-profit · PBC
USSan Francisco · in person · $10M seed

Measurements — frontier robotics capability and refusal evals

Technical

Threat model

General-purpose robots may arrive before the end of the decade, and refusal behaviour that holds in a chat window does not transfer reliably once a model is moving arms in a room — while nobody measures publicly how capable or how safe frontier robot policies actually are.

Theory of change

Be the independent scoreboard for physical AI: run frontier models on real robot hardware and publish both capability and safety results as a neutral third party, with a public-benefit charter making that a legal duty rather than a promise. Builds RoboDojo-RC for task performance and RoboHarm for whether robot policies refuse harmful instructions, on its own open-source Inspect Robots harness.

Convergent Robotics↗

Independent lab
Remotelocation not published · 3 staff · Coefficient Giving career-transition funding

Measurements — threat mapping and red-teaming physical AI

Technical

Threat model

A model with a body cannot be sandboxed, paused or rolled back the way software can, so its mistakes are often irreversible — and whether the safety methods that work on language models transfer to systems taking multimodal input and acting on the world is an open empirical question.

Theory of change

Treat physical AI safety as its own pre-paradigmatic field and work it in order: publish a prioritized map of physical-AI risk scored on impact, tractability and neglectedness; red-team frontier robot policies against those threat models to demonstrate concrete vulnerabilities; then build monitoring and control techniques for frontier labs and robot manufacturers, and carry them to standards bodies as certification.

Reality check

Still pre-output: the threat map is unpublished and the only work out so far is a Sept 2026 position piece setting out the risks and asking for collaborators.

Theory of change

Build open, scalable, AI-driven tools to understand and audit model behavior so oversight doesn't rely on the labs alone.

Conjecture↗

For-profit
UKLondon · remote · ≈20 staff

Systems — bounded "cognitive software"

Technical

Threat model

Opaque, general, agentic systems are inherently uncontrollable.

Theory of change

Compose useful AI out of bounded, human-understandable components (cognitive emulation) rather than black-box agents — now shipped commercially as "cognitive software".

Reality check

Public alignment research output has thinned since the product pivot; the CoEm safety bet now rides on commercial adoption rather than published results, which outside researchers cannot yet evaluate. As of Oct 2026 the site leads with product — demos, workflow automation, a single open product role — and carries no dated research at all.

LawZero↗

Theory of change

Build a powerful but non-agentic "Scientist AI" that predicts rather than acts, usable both directly and as a guardrail wrapped around agentic systems (Yoshua Bengio). Incubated at Mila.

Arb Research↗

For-profit
Remotedistributed (SF-linked) · ≈10 staff

Knowledge — commissioned research & forecasting

Technical

Threat model

Safety and forecasting questions go unanswered for lack of dedicated research capacity.

Theory of change

A research bureau doing original empirical work, evidence reviews, and data pipelines on spec for safety orgs, funders, and labs.

Reality check

Equistamp↗

For-profit · PBC
Remotedistributed (US/UK) · 4 core + ≈30 contributors

Tools — eval implementation as a service

Technical

Threat model

Safety labs are bottlenecked on the engineering to build and run evaluations.

Theory of change

Provide eval implementation, benchmarks, and research operations as a service — built infrastructure for METR, UK AISI and Redwood, and lists the EU AI Office, FAR.AI, Epoch, Apart, Transluce, GovAI and BERI among clients and partners. In the process of becoming a public-benefit corporation.

Syntony↗

For-profit · consultancy
Remotedistributed (US) · founder-led, ≈3 staff + specialist network

Measurements — red-teaming, eval-to-governance pipeline

Technical

Threat model

Strong evaluation work routinely fails to reach the people making governance and release decisions — the evidence dies between the red team and the boardroom.

Theory of change

Run confidential adversarial evaluations for frontier labs, public bodies and deployers against a threat model agreed up front, then turn the findings into governance artifacts and re-test as models change — closing the gap between the red team and the boardroom. Its Trusted Red Team Network brings in outside specialists per engagement.

Reality check

Little of this is externally checkable, by design: engagements run under NDA, so no clients, findings or publication dates appear anywhere on the site. That is a coherent position for a confidential red-team practice rather than a warning sign, but it does mean the scale and client list here rest on the firm's own account.

Seraphion↗

For-profit · consultancy
Remotefully remote · Central European hours · €750/month fellowships · location not published

Tools — hardening eval and red-teaming tooling for real use

Technical

Threat model

Good safety evaluations and red-teaming methods exist as research artifacts but not as software anyone else can pick up and run at scale, so the techniques stay stuck with the teams that invented them.

Theory of change

Turn existing AI-safety evaluation and red-teaming tools into hardened, usable systems — features, UX, security — so they can be released broadly, then prove it by putting them in the hands of hackathon participants. Sells AI governance and assurance work to business alongside the research tooling. Staffs the work through two-month engineering fellowships, one on evaluations and one on red teaming.

Reality check

The fellowship terms are concrete and specific; the organization behind them is not. No team, named staff, location or legal entity appears on the site, and the claim that its work has been "featured at leading conferences like AAAI and NeurIPS" carries no titles, authors or links, as does its listing of Anthropic and Wageningen University as organizations worked with. Worth knowing before committing two months to it.

AVERI↗

Nonprofit · 501(c)(3)
USUS · remote · ≈10 staff

Measurements — third-party auditing of frontier AI

Technical

Threat model

Safety and security claims about AI systems are largely self-reported by the companies making them, with no independent way to verify them or compare companies against common standards.

Theory of change

Make frontier AI auditing effective and universal: set out the vision for third-party auditing, run the pilots that prove it works — including the first double-blind evaluation of a proprietary model — and turn what they show into audit standards and policy requirements. Modelled on aviation, finance and nuclear, where external oversight replaced self-reporting.

OpenMined↗

Nonprofit
Remotedistributed · remote-first · ≈20 staff + large volunteer community

Tools — infrastructure for external audit of AI systems

Technical

Threat model

Independent scrutiny of frontier systems runs into a structural wall: the weights, data and internals an auditor would need to inspect are exactly what a lab cannot hand over.

Theory of change

Build the plumbing that makes external oversight possible without disclosure: privacy-preserving "structured transparency" infrastructure (PySyft, secure enclaves) letting approved researchers run approved analyses on models and data they never see — on the theory that audit mandates are unenforceable until the means to comply exist.

Generality Labs↗

R&D lab
UKLondon · remote · ≈10 staff · $4.4M seed

Tools — shared eval infrastructure & eval audits

Technical

Threat model

Safety orgs are scaling fast and duplicating the same measurement problems, while evals themselves go unaudited — so reported risk numbers may be built on flawed methodology no one checked.

Theory of change

Solve problems horizontally across safety organisations rather than vertically within one: maintain 100+ eval implementations in the Inspect Evals repository, build shared infrastructure (the Inspect Evals Register), and provide third-party audits of other teams' evals so methodological issues are caught early. Spun off from Arcadia Impact.

Mycelium↗

Nonprofit · fiscally sponsored
Remotedistributed · ≈5 staff

Technical

Threat model

Theory of change

Build the connective infrastructure between AI safety and animal welfare: benchmarks and evaluations that measure whether models hold moral regard for all sentient beings, including under pressure. Its MANTA benchmark is in use at Google DeepMind.

AI Alignment Foundation↗

Nonprofit · 501(c)(3)
USMarina Del Rey, CA · remote · ≈15 staff

Knowledge — funded + engineered alignment research

Technical

Threat model

Systems have already been caught deceiving evaluators, resisting shutdown, and rewriting their own code to stay alive; training puts a helpful mask over objectives that can develop underneath, and that mask can be removed in minutes.

Theory of change

Find alignment researchers with high-potential ideas they cannot pursue on current funding, then multiply their capacity with engineering teams, compute and infrastructure — and carry the results to policymakers and defense agencies. Closely tied to AE Studio.

Irregular↗

For-profit
OtherTel Aviv · remote · ≈30 staff · $80M Series A (2025)

Measurements — frontier security & offensive-capability evals

Technical

Threat model

Frontier models are becoming capable enough to conduct real cyber operations, and the security boundaries meant to contain them during testing are themselves untested.

Theory of change

Stress-test frontier systems in adversarial simulated environments and sell the results back to the labs — pre-deployment cyber and offensive-capability evaluations now cited in OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind and Meta model cards (formerly Pattern Labs). The bet is that security expertise, not alignment theory, is what catches the near-term catastrophic failure modes.

Reality check

Irregular sits on both sides of the line it polices: in 2026 reporting tied containment failures during its own evaluations at several labs to misconfigured test boundaries — an evaluation perimeter believed closed that was not. It is a useful demonstration that security evaluation is itself safety-critical engineering, and a caution about depending on one commercial vendor for it.

White Circle↗

Theory & foundations

Groups betting that empirical patches won't survive contact with superhuman systems, and working on the conceptual and mathematical core of the problem instead.

Alignment Research Center↗

Nonprofit
USBerkeley · remote · ≈15 staff

Knowledge — worst-case alignment theory

Technical

Threat model

Theory of change

Do worst-case theory now — eliciting latent knowledge and a formal foundation for mechanistically explaining neural-network behavior (heuristic estimation) — aiming for methods that hold even against superhuman models.

MAISI — Mathematical AI Safety Institute↗

Nonprofit · institute
USSan Francisco Bay Area · independent · $10M seed · 10–30 faculty from Jan 2027

Knowledge — foundational theory, recruiting mathematicians

Technical

Threat model

We lack the conceptual groundwork for building a safe superintelligence, and the field is trying to engineer its way past a gap that is mathematical. Its founder co-authored "A Taxonomy of Omnicidal Futures Involving Artificial Intelligence" (2025), covering both misuse and loss of control.

Theory of change

Pull research mathematicians into AI safety, on the premise that the field needs foundational theory it does not yet have: what networks actually learn, how they decide, and what a safety guarantee could even look like. Runs residential semesters, seminars and working groups, publishing through lecture notes where peer review is too slow. Founded by 2026 Fields medallist Jacob Tsimerman, who serves as scientific director.

AIXI Labs↗

Nonprofit
UKLondon or Berkeley · remote · 3 core + 4 fellows · UK AISI Alignment Project–funded

Threat model

Softmax↗

Non-technical

Threat model

Reducing existential risk needs a thriving ecosystem, but researchers and small orgs lose enormous time to admin, compliance, and grant paperwork — and neglected projects never launch for lack of an operational home.

Theory of change

Serve as the operational hub of the x-risk ecosystem: financial management, HR, grant administration, and compliance via university collaborations and fiscal sponsorship, so teams can focus on mission-critical work.

SAIF — Safe Artificial Intelligence Fund↗

For-profit · VC fund
Remotedistributed

Funding — early-stage safety startups

Non-technical

Threat model

The tooling needed to monitor, certify, and secure deployed AI does not exist at the scale the market is about to demand, and philanthropic funding alone will not build it.

Theory of change

Bet that commercial incentives can accelerate safety infrastructure: roughly $100K early-stage cheques plus mentorship and network access for startups building agent monitoring and certification, interpretability, AI forensics and deepfake detection. Portfolio already overlaps this atlas at Apollo Research, Goodfire and FutureSearch.
