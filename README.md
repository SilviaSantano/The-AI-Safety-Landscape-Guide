# The AI Safety Landscape — A working atlas

https://silviasantano.github.io/The-AI-Safety-Landscape-Guide/

Visual atlas of the organizations working to make advanced AI safe. It arranges active orgs by **where they apply leverage**, from inside the frontier labs outward to funding and public pressure, and colors them by where they sit.  
Each card primarily names the **threat model** it is built around, its **theory of change**, and the **main output the world gets** if the org succeeds: knowledge, measurements, tools, techniques & standards, or systems on the technical side; policy, talent, funding, or public pressure elsewhere, along with other relevant details. 

The goal is to show the shape of the field: who does what, to what end, and where the public record complicates the story, rather than to list every single org that exists.

It was created because the field is changing very rapidly and it is hard to keep track of all organizations, what they do and why. It is a tool I had wished existed but, although similar guides are available, I could not find anything in this visual form that incorporates all relevant information in one place, so I did it.

Last reviewed: **August 2026**


## How it's organized

Eight clusters, ordered by proximity to the models themselves:

1. **Inside the frontier labs** — safety work embedded where the most capable models are built.
2. **Independent empirical labs** — hands-on alignment, control, interpretability, and evaluation on real models, outside the frontier labs.
3. **Theory & foundations** — conceptual and mathematical work betting empirical patches won't survive superhuman systems.
4. **Talent, training & field-building** — the researcher pipeline and community hubs.
5. **Governance & policy research** — translating technical risk into policy, plus strategy/forecasting shops and the industry standards body.
6. **Advocacy & public mobilization** — groups treating public and political will as the binding constraint.
7. **National & governmental bodies** — the state's own capacity to test and regulate frontier systems.
8. **Funding & field infrastructure** — the money that decides which of the above exist.

The legend at the top is interactive. Filter by **location**, **output type**, **technical-work level**, and **org type**. Within a group, selections combine as **OR**; across groups, as **AND**. No selection in a group means "show all."

## Sources, provenance and accuracy

Reviewed and fact-checked against organizations' own sites and recent reporting. Seeded from the community "Map of AI Existential Safety" and cross-checked against the AISafety.com field map.  

Threat-model and theory-of-change lines are condensed characterizations, not official statements. Please verify against each org's own site before citing.

This is a VERY fast-moving field. Orgs launch, rename, merge, relocate, and pivot on a scale of weeks, so some entries are inevitably out of date the moment they're published, surely some of this information already is. I'll keep updating the atlas regularly.  
All of this is assembled to the best of my knowledge and any mistakes are my own. Corrections are welcome, and apologies for whatever slipped through!
